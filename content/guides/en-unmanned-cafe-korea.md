At an unmanned café in Korea you do four things yourself: choose, pay at the kiosk, collect your drink, and tidy up when you leave. Nobody greets you at the door, and nobody needs to. The first visit can still feel strange if you are used to ordering from a person, so this guide goes through it slowly, using Mingle, a self-service dessert café near Konkuk University in Seoul, as the example. Every store has its own setup, but the logic is usually the same.

## What "unmanned" actually means

In Korean these places are called 무인카페 (mu-in kape), which literally means "no-person café". It does not mean nobody looks after the place. At Mingle, the store is checked, restocked and cleaned every day. What you will not find is someone standing behind a counter.

That changes a few things. You cannot ask a barista for a recommendation, so you read labels instead, and payment happens on a screen rather than at a till. If something spills, the next person sees it unless you deal with it. In return you get a place that is open when most other places have closed, where you can sit for hours without anyone wondering when you will leave.

## Before you go

Two small things make the first visit easier.

Check your payment. Mingle's kiosk accepts cards and mobile payment only, with no cash. Cards issued abroad sometimes fail, and whether yours works depends on the company that issued it. If you have only just arrived in Korea, carry a second card the first time.

Save the address in both formats. Korean addresses have a road-name version and an older lot-number version, and map apps sometimes find one but not the other. For Mingle these are 39, Neungdong-ro 13-gil and 10-1 Hwayang-dong, both in Gwangjin-gu. The [English visit page](/en/visit.html) lists them along with the nearest subway station.

## Step by step, from the door to the bin

### Walk in

At Mingle the door is open 24 hours, and there is no reception desk. Just inside the entrance there is a QR code for Mingle Pass, the café's stamp card.

If you have an account, scanning it while logged in adds one stamp per day, and ten stamps get you a drink. You can skip this on a first visit.

### Choose your dessert

Desserts sit in two places. The chilled showcase holds slices of cake, puddings and cup desserts. The shelves hold things that keep at room temperature: cookies, brownies, financiers and madeleines. Each one is wrapped individually, so you can take one to your seat or put it in your bag for later.

The line-up changes, so the [menu page](/menu.html) shows the regular selection, while the showcase shows what is actually there today.

### Order at the kiosk

Drinks are ordered on the kiosk screen, which has an English option. Choose your drink, add any dessert you picked up, and pay by card or phone. The regular drinks include americano, café latte, cold brew, iced vanilla latte, milk tea, peach iced tea and a few fruit ades.

### Pick up your drink

Once you have paid, the coffee machine in the store makes your drink right away. Wait by the pickup area until it is ready. If you want to see the order of things before you go, the Korean [store page](/store.html) lays out the same three steps: choose, pay, pick up.

### Sit, or take it with you

Mingle has window seats with power outlets, ordinary tables and sofa seats, and there is no time limit. Stay for ten minutes or study through the night. Taking everything to go is just as normal.

### Clear up before you leave

This is the one step that matters most in an unmanned café. Separate cups, lids and wrappers into the recycling bins, wipe up anything you spilled, and push your chair back. Without staff, the state of the room is what tells the next person whether this is a good place.

## When something goes wrong

Machines do have bad days. If the kiosk freezes, the coffee machine stops halfway, or the item you wanted is gone, do not try to fix it by opening anything. Take a photo and send it through Mingle's KakaoTalk channel. There is nobody to answer a phone call in a self-service store, but messages with a photo are the quickest way to get something checked.

If you paid and did not get your drink, include the time of payment in your message. If you have a question about a receipt or a charge, the same channel works.

The sold-out labels and the store status shown on Mingle's website are updated by hand after the operator checks the store. They are not live readings, so now and then they will be a little behind.

For anything urgent, such as feeling unsafe or seeing someone hurt, call 112 for police or 119 for fire and ambulance first, and report to the café afterwards.

## Small courtesies that keep it working

A self-service café only stays pleasant when the people using it look after it. A few unwritten rules:

- Keep your voice down late at night, when people are often studying or working.
- Take your belongings with you if you step out for more than a minute.
- Do not save seats for long periods with a bag or laptop when the room is busy.
- If you see a mess you did not make, a quick message with a photo helps more than you would think.

The details differ from store to store, but once you have done it once, the basic routine carries over. ## FAQ

### Can I pay with cash at an unmanned café in Korea?

At Mingle, no. The kiosk accepts cards and mobile payment only. Other stores have their own rules, so check the kiosk before you order.

### Does the kiosk have an English option?

Mingle's kiosk does. Kiosks in other stores vary, so look for a language button on the first screen.

### Is anyone working at an unmanned café?

Nobody stands at a counter, but someone does look after the place. At Mingle the store is restocked and cleaned daily, and messages sent through the KakaoTalk channel are checked.

### What should I do if my drink does not come out?

Take a photo of the screen or machine and send it through the KakaoTalk channel, with the time you paid. Do not try to open the machine.

### How long can I stay?

At Mingle there is no time limit, and the café is open 24 hours. Just clear your table when you leave.
