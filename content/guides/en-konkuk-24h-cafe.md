If you need a café near Konkuk University that is still open at 2 a.m., Mingle in Hwayang-dong is one option. It is a self-service dessert café that runs 24 hours a day, every day of the year, with no staff on site. You can study as long as you like, pay by card at a kiosk, and pick up a boxed dessert on the way home. This guide covers what international students usually want to know before the first visit.

## Why a 24-hour café matters in your first semester

Your first months near Konkuk rarely run on a normal clock. Your body might still be on your home time zone, and your family calls when it is evening for them and the middle of the night for you. A group project meets at 10 p.m. because that is the only hour everyone is free. Then exam week arrives and your studio room starts to feel very small.

For those hours it helps to have one place you know will be open, lit and quiet. Mingle is roughly 30 pyeong (around 100 square metres), which is generous for a neighbourhood café. There are window seats with power outlets, regular tables and sofa seats. Nobody will ask you to leave, and nobody will check how long you have been sitting there.

## Finding Mingle from Konkuk Univ. Station

Mingle is within walking distance of Konkuk Univ. Station (Line 2 and Line 7), towards Hwayang-dong. Korean addresses come in two formats, and it helps to have both:

- Road name address: 39, Neungdong-ro 13-gil, Gwangjin-gu, Seoul
- Lot number address: 10-1 Hwayang-dong, Gwangjin-gu, Seoul

If one address does not show up in your map app, try the other. Our [visit page](/en/visit.html) has both, and the Korean [store page](/store.html) has direct links to Naver Map and KakaoMap, which are usually the easier choice for walking directions in Seoul.

The "13-gil" part means the café is on a side street that branches off Neungdong-ro, not on the main road itself. If you are walking along the big road looking for the number, you will probably walk past it. Follow the map route into the side streets instead.

## Ordering and paying without Korean

Nothing at Mingle requires you to speak Korean.

Desserts are in the fridge and on the shelves: cakes, puddings and cup desserts are chilled, while cookies, brownies, financiers and madeleines sit at room temperature. Drinks are ordered at the kiosk, which has an English option, and the coffee machine makes them on the spot. The regular menu includes americano, café latte, cold brew, Jeju matcha latte, milk tea and a few fruit ades. The [menu page](/menu.html) lists what is usually stocked, but the fridge on the day is always the most accurate.

The kiosk takes cards and mobile payment. It does not take cash. Some cards issued outside Korea may be declined, depending on the card company. If you have just arrived, bring a second card as a backup on your first visit, just in case.

Because there is no staff, the store status on our website is updated by the operator after checking the store in person. It is not a live sensor. Sold-out items are also marked by hand, so treat them as a rough guide.

## Studying late: a few house habits

There is no time limit at Mingle. Bring your charger, take a window seat and stay through the night if you need to. A few habits keep it comfortable for everyone:

- Keep calls short and quiet, or step outside. Sound carries at 3 a.m.
- If you leave your seat for a while, take your laptop and valuables with you. There is no staff watching the room.
- When you leave, separate cups and packaging into the recycling bins and push your chair back in.

If something is broken, out of stock or messy, tell us through the KakaoTalk channel. Nobody answers the phone in an unmanned store, but messages with a photo are checked quickly. In an emergency, call 112 for police or 119 for fire and ambulance.

There is also a small loyalty stamp called Mingle Pass. Scan the QR code in the store (inside the entrance, next to the kiosk or on the tables) while logged in, and you get one stamp per day. Ten stamps get you a drink.

## Meeting people around Konkuk

Mingle also runs a small neighbourhood community on its website. The board is mostly in Korean, but you can post in English. It is a good place to ask practical questions, like where to fix a laptop or how phone plans work, or to look for a study partner or someone to practise Korean with. Personal contact details such as phone numbers or messenger IDs cannot be posted, so plans are made on the board itself.

Members can also open meetups. A first meetup is checked once by the operator before it goes public. First meetings happen only at Mingle or another public place agreed in advance. Asking for contact details, and meetups for sales, investment or dating, are not allowed.

We are preparing three programmes with newcomers in mind. Korean Table is a half-Korean, half-English conversation table. Global Dessert Night is an evening about the sweets people grew up with. New in Konkuk is for people who have just moved here and are still working out banks, phone plans, clinics and housing.

None of these has held its first session yet. We will set dates once enough people have shown interest, and the [Global page](/global.html) has more on each.

If you are new to self-service cafés in general, the step-by-step guide on [how to use an unmanned café in Korea](/guide/en-unmanned-cafe-korea.html) walks through everything from the door to the bins.

If one of those programmes sounds like something you would come to, leave a one-line note on the board in English: which programme, and which evenings suit you. That is how the first dates get set.

## FAQ

### Is there a café near Konkuk University that is open 24 hours?

Mingle in Hwayang-dong is open 24 hours a day, every day, as a self-service café. It is within walking distance of Konkuk Univ. Station on Lines 2 and 7.

### Can I pay with a foreign credit card?

The kiosk takes cards and mobile payment, but some cards issued outside Korea may be declined depending on the card company. Cash is not accepted.

### Is there a time limit for studying?

No. You can stay as long as you like. Window seats have power outlets.

### Do I need to speak Korean?

No. The kiosk has an English option, and the community board accepts posts in English.

### Are there language exchange events at Mingle?

Korean Table and Global Dessert Night are being prepared but have not started yet. Leave a note on the board if you are interested, and we will set dates once enough people have signed up.
