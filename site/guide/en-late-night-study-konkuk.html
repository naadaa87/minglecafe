Where to study late at night near Konkuk University

When the campus reading room is full, or the café you like has started stacking its chairs, there are three realistic places to study late near Konkuk University: a campus study space, a paid study café, or a café that stays open all night. Mingle, in Hwayang-dong, is the third kind. It is a self-service dessert café, open 24 hours a day, with no time limit on its seats. This guide compares the three fairly, then explains how a long night at Mingle works.

What are the late-night study options near Konkuk?

There are three main kinds, and each suits a different sort of night.

Campus study spaces are quiet and cost nothing if you are a student. Their hours and entry rules differ from building to building and often change around exams, so read your university's notices instead of assuming.

Study cafés, written 스터디카페 in Korean, are not really cafés at all. You pay for time, by the hour or with a pass, and get a desk in a room where people barely whisper. If you need silence and a fixed desk, this is the closest thing to a library. Each one sets its own hours and prices, so check before you count on one.

All-night cafés work the ordinary way: you buy a drink and sit down. There is some background noise, you can eat, and nobody assigns you a seat. Many ordinary cafés close in the late evening, so far fewer are still open after midnight.

No option is better than the others. A night of memorising formulas may belong in a study café. A night of writing, with a snack and music in your earphones, may belong in a café.

What should I find out before choosing a spot?

Start with the closing time. It is frustrating to unpack everything and then hear that the place is about to close. Before you commit to a place for the night, check:

  • the real closing time, including last orders
  • whether there is a power outlet within reach of the seat you want
  • whether eating is allowed
  • how quiet the room is expected to be
  • how you will get home afterwards, or where you will wait for the first train

The last one is easy to forget in the evening and hard to ignore a few hours later.

How a night at Mingle works

You walk in, order at a kiosk and stay as long as you need. Mingle is at 39, Neungdong-ro 13-gil, Gwangjin-gu, within walking distance of Konkuk Univ. Station (Lines 2 and 7) in the direction of Hwayang-dong. There is no staff on site and no closing time. The visit page lists both address formats, which helps when a map app refuses one of them.

The kiosk can be switched to English, and it takes cards and mobile payment but not cash. Cards issued outside Korea are sometimes declined, depending on the issuer, so carry a backup if you have only just arrived. Drinks are made by the coffee machine in the store. Desserts are individually wrapped: cake slices, puddings and cup desserts in the fridge, and cookies, brownies, financiers and madeleines on the shelves. The menu page shows the regular line-up, and the step-by-step guide to unmanned cafés covers the kiosk.

The room is about 30 pyeong, a Korean unit that comes to roughly 100 square metres. There are three kinds of seat, and it helps to match the seat to the task:

  • The window seats are the ones with power outlets. Take one for laptop work, or for anything that will outlast your battery.
  • Tables give you room to put notes and a textbook side by side.
  • Sofa seats suit reading better than writing.

This guide only states what we can confirm. For anything else your night depends on, such as Wi-Fi, check the visit page or ask through the KakaoTalk channel before you plan a deadline around it.

Is it okay to stay all night?

Yes. Mingle sets no time limit, so you can stay from the evening until the first trains run. Because nobody is on duty, the room depends on the people using it, and a few habits keep it workable.

Start with an order at the kiosk, as you would in any café. Keep phone calls outside, or very short, and use earphones for anything with sound. Mingle is a café, so there is no silence rule, and people at the next table may be chatting. On nights when you need complete quiet, a study café is the better choice.

Take your laptop and wallet with you whenever you step out, because no one is watching your seat. When you leave, sort your cups and packaging into the bins, push your chair in, and unplug your charger.

If a machine stops or something runs out, send a message with a photo through the KakaoTalk channel. Nobody can answer a phone in an unmanned store. For a real emergency, the numbers are 112 (police) and 119 (fire and ambulance).

Pacing a long night

All-nighters tend to go wrong in the middle, not at the start. A few things help.

Split your order. Have your coffee first and something lighter later, such as one of the fruit ades on the regular menu. Buy a wrapped dessert early and open it when your concentration dips, so you do not have to get up in the middle of a thought.

Put the hardest task first. Anything that needs real thinking should come early; save reviewing and formatting for the hours when you are slower.

Decide your exit before you start: either a time you will go home, or the first train. The nights with no planned ending are the ones that drift.

If you would rather not study alone, Mingle is preparing a neighbourhood study club (화양동 스터디 클럽) and a weekday afternoon co-working session called Work Sprint. Neither has held its first session yet, and dates will be set once enough people have shown interest on the neighbourhood board. For the unwritten rules that apply in any café here, see studying in cafés in Korea.

Before your next late night, save 39, Neungdong-ro 13-gil in your map app and walk the route once in daylight. A side street is much easier to find the second time.

FAQ

Is there somewhere open 24 hours to study near Konkuk University?

Mingle, a self-service dessert café in Hwayang-dong, is open 24 hours a day, every day, and has no time limit on seats. You can walk there from Konkuk Univ. Station, which is on Lines 2 and 7.

Does Mingle have power outlets for laptops?

Yes. The window seats at Mingle have power outlets. If you need one for the whole night, take a window seat when you arrive.

Is Mingle quiet enough to study?

Mingle is a café, not a silent study room, so there is no rule against talking at the tables. Bring earphones, or choose a study café on nights when you need complete silence.

Can I pay at Mingle with a card from my home country?

It depends on the card issuer. Mingle's kiosk accepts cards and mobile payment, and a card from abroad may or may not go through. Cash is not accepted, so carry a second card if you can.

What is the difference between a study café and a regular café in Korea?

A study café (스터디카페) charges for time and gives you a quiet desk, while a regular café sells drinks and lets you sit. Mingle is a regular café that is open all night and has no time limit.
